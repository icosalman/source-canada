How we read the research

Rigour you can check.

Biomolecular science moves fast and claims travel faster. We slow down, show our working and separate what is measured from what is merely suggested.

Learn more about us or read the FAQ.

  1. 01

    Study design comes first

    Before discussing a result, we say where it came from: cell culture, animal models or human trials. Each says something different.

  2. 02

    Numbers, not adjectives

    We report sample sizes, doses, durations and effect sizes alongside findings, so you can judge how strong a result really is.

  3. 03

    Every quality claim has a test

    When we talk about identity or purity, we name the method behind it (HPLC, LC-MS, amino acid analysis) and what it can't detect.

  4. 04

    Dated and kept current

    Each article shows when it was published and last updated, and we revise it as new research changes the picture.

Independent research sources

Start with the evidence. Go beyond the summary.

Scientific information is only as useful as the sources behind it. When researching analytical compounds, reference methods, or emerging areas of biotechnology, we look to established scientific databases and publication records rather than relying on unsourced claims.

These independent resources can help you explore the underlying literature, verify published findings, and continue your own research.

  • 01

    PubMed (opens in a new tab)

    A comprehensive biomedical literature database maintained by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. Use it to find peer-reviewed research articles, abstracts, and related scientific publications.

    pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

  • 02

    PubMed Central (opens in a new tab)

    A free, full-text archive providing access to a broad collection of biomedical and life-science research publications.

    pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

  • 03

    ClinicalTrials.gov (opens in a new tab)

    A public database containing information about clinical studies from around the world, including study descriptions, recruitment information, and available results.

    clinicaltrials.gov

  • 04

    Crossref (opens in a new tab)

    An independent nonprofit organization that provides scholarly metadata and DOI registration services, making it easier to locate and connect published academic research.

    crossref.org
